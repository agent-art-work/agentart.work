# Workflow

## 2 Choose one question and its audience

For each idea, write:

1. What do I think?

2. Why does it matter to someone?

3. What example can we examine?

4. What would make me change my mind?

Start where an idea is central to the manifesto, matters to people, and leaves room for real disagreement. A popular AI topic may still be a poor fit.

## 4 Find the words people already use

Use everyday words to learn how people describe the problem. For Route A, this helps find a conversation. For Route B, it helps write an opening people understand; an existing discussion is not required.

- Human worth: `AI makes me feel useless`

- Skill: `why learn when AI can do it`

- Choice: `AI changed my plan`

- Intention: `AI agent pursuing its own goal`

- Credit: `who made it AI or me`

Try AI, agent, AI agent, and app names such as Claude, Gemini or ChatGPT. Also try the question without an AI word. Keep work, learning and daily life in the search alongside art.

### Use the same test for each community

1. Pick one manifesto question. Keep its meaning the same across places; adapt the wording to local conversation.

2. Spend up to 20 minutes per community. Try the everyday search words in step 4, then AI, Agent and app name variants. Save the queries and the exact links.

3. Read up to five relevant threads and their replies. Record how many contain reasons, examples or useful disagreement, plus the date of the latest relevant reply. If fewer exist, record the smaller sample.

4. Check rules for replies and new posts separately, including AI writing, links, self promotion, language, cost and time needed. For Route B, check whether people can find a new post and whether comments are open. Mark a blocked check as unknown.

5. Record **keep**, **more checks**, or **skip for this question** separately for A and B. Keep A when there is a suitable conversation to join. Keep B when the intended people can find and discuss an allowed original post. The two routes may need different places.

6. In a later test, use one example and one question for each route where allowed. Judge useful replies, counterexamples and continued exchange. Record views or reach if available; silence alone does not prove poor fit.

## 7 Learn from replies in both routes

For either route, respond to reasons and examples, then record:

- **Their point:** what did they actually say?

- **Their example:** what supports it?

- **What it changes:** wording, evidence, a claim, or nothing yet?

- **Next step:** ask more, test a work, revise, or move on?

A confused reply may show unclear wording. A strong counterexample may challenge a claim. Likes show attention; reasons and examples show what you can learn.

## 8 Start with a small comparison

1. Pick one question from step 1.

2. Compare three communities across different platforms using the same question and the test in step 5. Assess A and B separately.

3. Save the exact threads, search words, useful exchanges and access limits. For B, also save the planned format and how readers would find it.

4. Choose one allowed opening for A and one suitable place for B. They can be in the same community or different ones.

5. Prepare a reply for A and one original piece for B, each with an example and a clear question. Follow each place's writing rules. Plan the same review period for both.

6. After testing both routes, compare useful replies, different views, continued conversations and time spent. Record reach when available; if few people saw a post, test how it reaches readers before judging the idea.

For the first comparison, use **what each side contributed**. Screen Reddit, Substack and LessWrong for both routes, then choose a live thread for A and an allowed publishing place for B. A reader's access to someone else's Substack comments does not establish an audience for their own post. Then compare the skill question outside art. Test Agent intention separately with builders, using an observed choice and what would count against your interpretation.

**Save each test in one line:** manifesto idea → question and people → route A or B → exact community and thread or original post → rules and access → how readers find it → review period and reach if known → useful feedback → next step.
